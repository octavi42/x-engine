## 5. Thread hook — speed is product design, not inference

### Hook (post 1)

> shipping AI products this year taught me one thing about latency: speed is product design, not inference. four patterns i keep reusing across roadtosf and clio →

### Post 2 — make the wait the experience

> 1/ the loading screen IS the gameplay.
>
> roadtosf takes 4-6s to gen the episode arc with sonnet 4.6. instead of optimizing the model call, the first scene loads first: "you just landed at SFO, your co-founder is texting you" — while sonnet finishes upstream.

### Post 3 — pre-fire the next thing

> 2/ pre-warm what's next while the user reads the current one.
>
> roadtosf fires the next episode + scene 0 image in the background while you walk the final scene of the current one. by the time you reach it, it's warm. zero perceived wait between episodes.

### Post 4 — open the conversation before the data arrives

> 3/ teaser narration covers the research call.
>
> clio narrates GPS locations under 3s. system prompt has a [TEASER] signal — agent opens with a captivating intro line while firecrawl pulls real-time research. data lands silently mid-sentence.

### Post 5 — never re-pay for slow ops on refresh

> 4/ never persist expensive-to-regenerate output to client storage.
>
> roadtosf burned ~$0.30/refresh in image regen because zustand stripped b64 dataUrls on rehydrate. fix: upload to vercel blob, persist a short URL. cheaper, survives deploys.

### Post 6 — CTA

> the pattern: latency you can't shrink, you can hide. design the wait, don't apologize for it.
>
> roadtosf.com if you want to see all four in one app.
