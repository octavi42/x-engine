**Outline (5 posts):**

1. Hook (above) — reframes "metadata-only" as the buyer's misread, not a compliance shortcut.
2. GDPR Article 4(1): identifiable includes indirect identifiers. Calendar attendee lists + Slack channel membership + doc last-editor stamps ARE personal data. No exemption for "we didn't read the message body."
3. What the DPIA actually asks: purpose, categories of data, retention, legal basis (Art 6), risk to rights + freedoms, mitigations. Not paperwork — the mitigations become schema constraints (row-level retention TTL, redacted actor IDs, opt-out per actor).
4. Build-time fixes that make the audit cheap: hash actor IDs at ingest (rehydrate only on export with a reversible key held by the customer), 90-day rolling window as default, no free-text ingest ever (only enum'd verbs from an allowlist).
5. Why this is the wedge, not the tax: customers with a working DPIA process buy faster than customers without. Selling to finance-as-cost-center in 100-400 FTE post-Series-B shops (the ICP) means procurement forwards the DPIA to legal on day 3 — either you have it or you're out.
