**Thread outline:**

1. Hook (above)
2. The problem: decomposing directed graphs into Hamiltonian cycles. Knuth solved the 3x3x3 case, verified up to 16x16x16 computationally, but had no general construction for all odd m.
3. The solve: Filip Stappers fed the problem to Claude Opus 4.6. 31 guided explorations over ~1 hour. Claude independently recognized the underlying structure as a Cayley digraph from group theory — that insight unlocked the general solution.
4. The catch: Claude found the construction but couldn't prove it was correct. Knuth wrote the formal mathematical proof himself.
5. The nuance: this wasn't a single prompt. 31 explorations with human steering. Stappers redirected when Claude lost track, prompted it to document intermediate results. Interactive, not autonomous.
6. The follow-up: others used GPT + Claude together to find simpler constructions for both odd and even cases. Dr. Kim Morrison formalized the proof in Lean.
7. Takeaway: the most productive AI use isn't replacement or avoidance. It's guided exploration with human verification. Knuth — the person who literally wrote the book on algorithms — demonstrated this.
