## 5. Thread — four interpretation-science rules the dossier bakes in before the model opens its mouth

### Hook (post 1)

> the thing I care most about in Clio isn't the voice. it's the rules the model follows before it opens its mouth
>
> four interpretation-science rules that the dossier bakes in before a single syllable is generated

### Post 2 — one sentence theme

> rule 1: one sentence theme per place
>
> the story is answering one question, not reciting the article
>
> if the dossier can't name the theme in a line, the segment doesn't get written

### Post 3 — people over dates

> rule 2: people over dates
>
> "the architect's brother drowned in the canal" beats "built 1872" every time
>
> dates anchor, people carry. the model is told to open on a person whenever the source supports it

### Post 4 — pointable anchor

> rule 3: every claim has a pointable anchor
>
> the dome, the capital on the second column, the rust stain by the gate
>
> the look-at ring lands on the thing. abstract adjectives without a referent are rewritten or dropped

### Post 5 — hedge by source confidence

> rule 4: hedge by source confidence
>
> "the plaque says" vs "we know" vs "a tour guide I trust claims"
>
> the model can't keep that straight on its own, so the dossier labels confidence per fact and the prompt binds the hedge to the label
