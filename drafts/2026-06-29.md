## 5. Thread hook with outline — "mystery is the product" (roadtosf design principle)

### Hook (post 1)

> "mystery is the product" is the design principle I keep coming back to on roadtosf
>
> the menu of choices doesn't exist. the player types whatever, the LLM picks a silent flavor tag, the next scene reacts
>
> 4 posts on why hiding the system beats showing it

### Post 2 — explicit menus kill the magic

> tried explicit menus first
>
> "do you (a) call your investor (b) tell your co-founder (c) walk out". felt like a choose-your-own-adventure book from 1984
>
> the player sees the seams. the LLM stops feeling like a co-author and starts feeling like a state machine

### Post 3 — silent flavor-tag extraction

> what works: free-text input + silent tag extraction
>
> Haiku 4.5 reads the typed reply, picks 1 flavor tag from a fixed list (cautious, brash, idealist, ruthless), no UI feedback
>
> the next scene tilts on that tag. the player feels seen without seeing the rules

### Post 4 — the cost of mystery

> cost of mystery: you lose the safety rail
>
> "did the LLM get my intent?" anxiety creeps in. some players type 3-word replies because they're not sure the system parses anything
>
> mitigation: tiny visual confirmation. a glint, a beat shift, a one-word echo. enough to say "got it"

### Post 5 — close

> the menu kills the dream. the dream is the product
>
> if your agent needs a dropdown, the agent isn't ready
>
> roadtosf.com if you want to feel the difference
