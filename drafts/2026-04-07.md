### Thread outline:
1. Hook (above)
2. The headline proposals: subsidized 32-hour workweek with no pay cut, public wealth fund giving citizens a stake in AI growth, shift tax burden from labor to capital — including a "robot tax" on automated labor
3. The revenue side: raise capital gains taxes at the top, increase corporate income tax, explore new levies on sustained AI-driven returns. employers boost retirement matches, cover more healthcare, subsidize childcare
4. The contradiction: OpenAI is simultaneously IPO-ing at $300B+, generating $2B/month revenue, and proposing regulations on the industry it dominates. the company displacing labor is writing the policy playbook for displaced workers
5. What's missing: no mention of open-source, no mention of compute concentration, no acknowledgment that 4 companies control 80% of AI funding. the policy assumes broad market competition that doesn't exist
6. The real question: is this genuine policy thinking or preemptive PR for the backlash that comes when AI unemployment data gets real?
